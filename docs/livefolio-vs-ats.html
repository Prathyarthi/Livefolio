Internal positioning brief

Livefolio vs Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, and LinkedIn Recruiter

Those products run hiring as a process system. Livefolio runs hiring as proof of work for one job. The unique factor is not another pipeline. It is evidence, pulled from real integrations, so the work is actually looked at.

Positioning: Greenhouse tells you whether the team followed a process. LinkedIn tells you whether a profile contains keywords. Livefolio tells you whether this applicant can show the work for this role — GitHub, live projects, writing, tenure.

1. What each product actually is

Greenhouse

Structured hiring ATS. Scorecards, interview kits, approvals, a huge HR integration marketplace. Built so every interviewer grades the same attributes. Candidate input is usually a parsed resume plus a long form.

Ashby

All-in-one ATS + CRM + scheduling + analytics for growth teams. Fast apply, AI matching against the JD, visual pipelines. Still resume-in, score-out. Strong at recruiter ops, not at verifying shipped work.

Lever

ATS + CRM. Relationship and nurture first. Recruiters source, drip, and convert a talent pool. The record is a person timeline. Proof of work is whatever is pasted into the resume or LinkedIn.

LinkedIn Recruiter / Wellfound

Marketplaces. Search everyone, InMail, Easy Apply. Optimized for reach and keywords, not for a job-scoped evidence packet. Livefolio deliberately does the opposite: only people who applied to this job.

2. Flow of execution

Same seven beats exist everywhere. What moves through them is different.

Them — resume operating system

  1. Open a req. Job + scorecard attributes live in the ATS.
  2. Attract. Career page, job boards, LinkedIn Easy Apply, agency upload.
  3. Apply. PDF resume is parsed into fields. Maybe knockout questions. Maybe a cover letter.
  4. Screen. Keyword search, AI JD match, years in a text box. Recruiter opens a resume viewer.
  5. Interview. Structured kits (Greenhouse) or flexible stages (Ashby/Lever). Scorecards on opinions.
  6. Decide. Debrief, offer, HRIS. Work samples are optional attachments.
  7. System of record. The resume PDF + notes. If the GitHub or live site dies later, nobody knows.

Integrations here are plumbing: Calendar, Slack, Workday, Checkr, Zoom. They move the candidate. They do not inspect the work.

Livefolio — evidence operating system

  1. Open a req. Job + structured requirements (skills, tenure, projects).
  2. Attract. Public job link. Apply with Livefolio — not Easy Apply emptiness.
  3. Apply. One click shares their Livefolio: experience, projects, live URLs, GitHub / Medium / LeetCode where connected, plus resume if they have one.
  4. Screen. Search only that job’s pool. Each requirement maps to evidence in their work. Pool coverage shows what the inbound set cannot prove.
  5. Look at the work. Open Livefolio, open the resume, open the live project. Compare 2–4 people on the same requirement rows.
  6. Move pipeline. Stages, shortlist, notes — enough to run a loop, not a full enterprise ATS.
  7. Share with hiring managers. They review the same evidence and links, not a keyworded PDF.

Integrations here are proof: GitHub, live sites, writing, problem-solving. They make the work inspectable.

4. How we are unique (say this, not the ATS checklist)

Evidence, not keywords

A Greenhouse knockout question is “Do you have 5 years of Python?” Livefolio checks dated work history and project tech stacks, then shows the proof row. Ashby’s AI score is still JD-text overlap. We attach the work.

Integrations that inspect work

Their integrations connect tools recruiters already own. Ours connect artifacts candidates already shipped: GitHub, live product URLs, writing, LeetCode, original resume. That is why a recruiter can actually look at the work.

Job-scoped, not a marketplace

LinkedIn Recruiter’s power is browsing everyone. That is also the spam and the keyword theater. Livefolio only searches people who applied to this role. High signal, no “hunt the network.”

Pool coverage

ATS analytics count funnel stages. Livefolio counts proof: which requirements nobody in this inbound pool can show. Hiring managers change the JD or the sourcing channel, not another keyword boolean.

5. What we should never pretend to be

Do not sell against Greenhouse on interview kits, Ashby on scheduling analytics, or Lever on nurture campaigns. Those are mature process products. Selling “we are a lighter ATS” makes us a worse Ashby. Selling “we make the work visible for this job” makes us a category they cannot clone without owning the candidate identity.
